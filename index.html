About the College

A pioneer of Muslim education in Sri Lanka.

Hameed Al Husseinie College began as a school named Al Madrasathul Khairiyyathul Islamiah. It was founded by Marhoom Sidde Lebbe, Marhoom Wapichi Marikkar and Ahmed Urabi Pasha.

At the opening ceremony on 15 November 1884 — the first Muslim school in Sri Lanka — Ahmed Orabi spoke on the importance of the new school to the country's Muslim community.

Many families were at first unwilling to join the modern education system of the 19th century. Most schools were run by Christian missionaries, and conservative parents feared that English education might draw their children away from their faith.

Siddi Lebbe therefore worked to establish separate schools for Muslims. That dream became real in November 1884 with the first Muslim English school in the country.

Our Heritage

From a pioneering school to a national institution.

In 1901, Noordeen Hadjiar, trustee of the Grand Mosque of Colombo, built a new building for the school with his own funds as a monument to the Silver Jubilee of Abdul Hamid II, the Ottoman Sultan. The school was renamed Al Madrasathul Hameediah.

In 1921 the school was again renamed Hameediah Boys' English School.

Members of the Ceylon Moslem Educational Society later acquired the property at 45 Husseiniya Street. The school eventually became known as Husseinie Boys' English School.

In 1961 the neighbouring schools were amalgamated under the name Hameed Al Husseinie Maha Vidyalaya by Badiudin Mahmud, Minister of Education.

A defining milestone On 26 July 1996, Hameed Al Husseinie College was elevated to National School status.

On 29 August 1972, government ministers Pieter Keuneman and Badiudin Mahmud visited the school and unveiled a new five-storey building block and a three-storey science section. Those buildings were completed in 1982.

Prime Minister Ranasinghe Premadasa declared them open on 3 June 1982, at the invitation of Minister of Education Ranil Wickremesinghe.

The school was officially granted college status in 1991 and renamed Hameed Al Husseinie College .

To mark the school's 125th year, a Rs. 5 stamp was released on 30 July 2009 at the college premises. A commemorative walk for the 140th year was held on 4 January 2025.

A living legacy.

Hameed Al Husseinie College is an Islamic national school for boys, offering Grades 1 to 13.

Alumni are known as Hameedians or Old Hameedians.

For more than a century, the institution has remained connected to the educational journey of generations of students.
